# Comparative Corporate Credit Analysis — LVMH, Richemont and BHP

**Assessment date:** 19 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Comparative issuer-level credit case study for a banking portfolio  
**Currency:** EUR for LVMH and Richemont; US dollars for BHP  
**Scope:** Public-disclosure analysis. This is not a formal credit rating, lender approval, investment recommendation or internal bank risk assessment.

---

## 1. Executive conclusion

The three companies illustrate materially different corporate credit profiles:

- **LVMH — diversified luxury platform:** broad brand and product diversification, strong operating cash flow and moderate net financial debt. The key risk is a cyclical slowdown in luxury demand, particularly the high earnings contribution from Fashion & Leather Goods.
- **Richemont — jewellery-led luxury specialist:** substantial net cash, very high jewellery margins and strong current trading momentum. The key risks are earnings concentration in jewellery, inventory intensity and the lower profitability of Specialist Watchmakers and Other.
- **BHP — large-scale resources producer:** strong operating cash generation, low reported net leverage and globally competitive assets. The key risks are commodity-price cyclicality, operational disruption and a substantial capital investment programme.

**Overall credit view:** All three display meaningful financial capacity, but the source of resilience differs. Richemont has the strongest reported net cash position relative to its own operating profile; BHP has low net debt relative to FY2026 underlying EBITDA; LVMH has a broad, diversified earnings base and significant liquidity, but carries net financial debt. These statements are issuer-specific observations, not a numerical ranking of credit quality.

A lender should not rank the companies by comparing raw euro and US-dollar balances or by treating their published EBITDA, operating profit, free cash flow and net debt definitions as interchangeable. The reporting periods also differ: LVMH FY2025/H1 2026, Richemont FY2026/H1 FY2027, and BHP FY2026/Q1 FY2027.

## 3. Business risk comparison

### LVMH

LVMH has the broadest business diversification of the three: fashion and leather goods, wines and spirits, perfumes and cosmetics, watches and jewellery, and selective retailing. FY2025 revenue was €80.807bn and profit from recurring operations was €17.755bn. Fashion & Leather Goods generated €37.770bn revenue and €13.209bn profit from recurring operations, approximately 74% of Group recurring operating profit. This creates a concentration within an otherwise diversified portfolio.

H1 2026 revenue declined 3% reported but increased 2% organically. Profit from recurring operations declined 4% to €8.691bn. This illustrates the distinction between organic demand trends and currency/perimeter effects.

**Credit strengths:** brand portfolio, scale, distribution reach, diversified product categories, high operating margin and significant cash generation.

**Credit vulnerabilities:** sensitivity to high-end discretionary spending, fashion-cycle execution, brand desirability, tourism flows, currency, tariffs and the outsized earnings contribution of Fashion & Leather Goods.

### Richemont

Richemont is more concentrated in luxury than LVMH and more concentrated in jewellery within its own profit pool. Jewellery Maisons generated €16.5bn sales and a 30.5% operating margin in FY2026. H1 FY2027 Jewellery Maisons sales grew 19% at constant exchange rates and achieved a 35.3% operating margin, or 33.4% excluding positive one-time items.

**Credit strengths:** leading jewellery maisons, strong pricing power and product identity, high segment margins, substantial net cash and strong recent operating momentum.

**Credit vulnerabilities:** high jewellery earnings concentration, inventory of €10.262bn at September 2026, elevated precious-metal costs, lower watchmaking margins and continued losses in Other. Inventory rotation improved to 16.1 months but remains a significant capital commitment.

### BHP

BHP has a capital-intensive, commodity-linked business model. Copper generated 54% of Group underlying EBITDA in FY2026, while iron ore generated 43%. Both businesses reported high segment margins. These assets benefit from scale and cost competitiveness but are exposed to global commodity prices, operating reliability and jurisdictional risk.

**Credit strengths:** large-scale and long-life assets, strong cost positions, geographic spread, significant cash generation, low reported net leverage and flexibility to adjust discretionary distributions.

**Credit vulnerabilities:** commodity price shocks, production disruptions, high sustaining and growth capex, project execution, mine closure obligations, safety and environmental liabilities, and joint-venture funding commitments.

## 4. Financial resilience and balance-sheet comparison

### LVMH — positive but moderate net debt

LVMH reported net financial debt of €6.857bn at FY2025 year-end and €8.245bn at 30 June 2026. H1 2026 cash and current available-for-sale financial assets were €12.202bn, with €10.8bn of undrawn confirmed credit lines. H1 operating free cash flow was €4.1bn.

The Group's liquidity is substantial, but the increase in net financial debt between FY2025 and H1 2026 should be monitored alongside distributions, investment, acquisition activity and cash-flow seasonality. Its broad portfolio and strong cash generation provide support, but the balance sheet is not in net cash.

### Richemont — strongest reported net cash buffer

Richemont reported net cash of €8.496bn at 31 March 2026 and €7.729bn at 30 September 2026. H1 FY2027 operating cash flow was €2.5bn. Its net cash position remains a major credit strength, even after a significant dividend payment and continued investment.

The balance-sheet buffer should be assessed with care: gross cash and investments, external borrowings, currency, legal-entity location and transfer restrictions matter. Inventory is material and cannot be treated as cash-equivalent liquidity.

### BHP — low leverage relative to current earnings

BHP reported net debt of US$8.694bn and underlying EBITDA of US$32.947bn at FY2026, resulting in reported net debt/underlying EBITDA of 0.3x. Net operating cash flow was US$21.778bn and free cash flow was US$9.8bn.

The ratio is strong but cyclical. FY2026 earnings benefited from higher realised prices for copper, iron ore and steelmaking coal. The analyst should stress-test a lower commodity-price environment and higher project expenditure rather than assume the reported leverage ratio is sustainable through the cycle.

## 5. Cash generation and capital allocation

| Issuer | Cash generation evidence | Main competing uses of cash |
|---|---|---|
| LVMH | H1 2026 operating free cash flow €4.1bn; FY2025 €11.333bn | Dividends, stores, manufacturing, brand investment, acquisitions and working capital |
| Richemont | H1 FY2027 operating cash flow €2.5bn; free cash flow €2.028bn | Dividends, share purchases, boutiques, manufacturing, inventory and strategic investment |
| BHP | FY2026 net operating cash flow US$21.778bn; free cash flow US$9.8bn | Capital and exploration expenditure, dividends, Samarco obligations, Jansen and other growth projects |

Cash generation quality differs. Luxury businesses must fund inventory, retail networks, brand investment and working capital. BHP must fund sustaining capital, large development projects, rehabilitation and closure obligations. In all three cases, the credit analyst should distinguish recurring operating cash flow from asset disposals, streaming arrangements, exceptional receipts and other non-recurring items.

## 7. Credit committee perspective

### Which issuer appears most resilient?

There is no defensible single winner without specifying the stress scenario.

- **Luxury-sector demand shock:** Richemont has the stronger net cash starting point, but its profit pool is more concentrated in jewellery and inventory is large. LVMH has greater product diversification but carries net financial debt and a material Fashion & Leather Goods concentration. A prolonged luxury downturn would test both.
- **Commodity-price shock:** BHP's 0.3x reported leverage provides a meaningful starting buffer, but the ratio could rise quickly if prices and volumes fall together while sustaining capex remains high.
- **Liquidity shock:** Richemont's net cash is a clear strength. LVMH also has substantial liquidity and confirmed lines. BHP's gross debt, cash, facility availability and maturity profile need to be examined in detail rather than inferred from net debt alone.
- **Capital expenditure shock:** BHP is most exposed to large project commitments and sustaining investment. Luxury groups have retail and manufacturing investment, but generally lower mining-style project intensity.

### Indicative lender posture

| Issuer | Preliminary stance | Primary monitoring focus |
|---|---|---|
| LVMH | Consider senior unsecured exposure subject to normal due diligence | Organic growth, Fashion & Leather Goods margins, net debt and liquidity |
| Richemont | Consider senior unsecured exposure; strongest net cash position of the three at latest reported date | Jewellery concentration, inventory rotation, net cash and capital returns |
| BHP | Consider senior unsecured exposure subject to through-cycle commodity stress | Commodity price deck, net debt, sustaining capex, projects and closure liabilities |

These are preliminary public-information views only. Actual limits, tenor, pricing, security and covenant structure require lender-specific information, entity analysis and internal approval.

## 8. Recommended comparative stress tests

A stronger banking portfolio should model each issuer under a sector-specific downside rather than apply the same generic EBITDA haircut to all three.

### LVMH — luxury demand recession

- Fashion & Leather Goods revenue down 15%;
- Wines & Spirits revenue down 10%;
- Selective Retailing margin compresses;
- Operating costs flex only partially;
- FX and tariffs remain adverse;
- Dividends and capex held at planned levels initially.

Track operating profit, cash conversion, net financial debt and liquidity headroom.

### Richemont — jewellery and inventory shock

- Jewellery sales down 15%;
- Jewellery gross margin compresses due to gold costs and lower pricing power;
- Specialist Watchmakers recovery reverses;
- Inventory rotation deteriorates from 16.1 to 19–20 months;
- Working capital absorbs cash;
- Dividends continue at the recent level.

Track segment profit, inventory, operating cash flow, net cash and cash conversion.

### BHP — commodity and operational shock

- Copper and iron ore realised prices decline materially;
- Copper production falls due to grade and operational disruption;
- Unit costs rise due to energy, labour and foreign exchange;
- Sustaining capex remains necessary;
- Growth projects continue with limited deferral;
- Dividends are flexed only after a defined cash buffer.

Track underlying EBITDA, operating cash flow, free cash flow, net debt/EBITDA and liquidity.

### Cross-issuer outputs

For each scenario, calculate:
1. Revenue and operating earnings;
2. EBITDA or issuer-defined operating earnings, with a reconciliation;
3. Working-capital movement;
4. Sustaining and growth capex;
5. Free cash flow under a clearly defined common analyst formula;
6. Dividends and other distributions;
7. Closing gross debt, cash and net debt/net cash;
8. Leverage and interest coverage using consistent definitions where possible;
9. Liquidity available to the borrower entity;
10. A narrative credit recommendation and key triggers.

## 9. Data limitations and diligence priorities

The public disclosures used do not provide all information required for a bank underwriting decision. Before assigning a formal internal grade or approving a facility, obtain:

- Borrower and guarantor legal structure, entity-level accounts and cash locations;
- Gross debt, maturity ladders, interest rates, security and committed undrawn facilities;
- Facility agreements, guarantees, covenants and covenant certificates;
- Cash restrictions, trapped cash, counterparty exposures and transferability;
- Lease and other fixed contractual commitments;
- Pension, litigation, tax, environmental and contingent liabilities;
- Monthly liquidity forecasts under base and downside cases;
- Management forecasts, budgets and capital-allocation plans;
- Current rating-agency reports and subsequent rating actions.

Do not invent PD, LGD, EAD, internal risk grades, covenant headroom or lender-specific default metrics from public financial statements.

## 10. Final comparative conclusion

The three issuers are credible candidates for a public-disclosure corporate credit portfolio because they test different analytical skills:

- LVMH tests diversified consumer and luxury brand analysis.
- Richemont tests concentrated high-margin jewellery economics, inventory and net cash analysis.
- BHP tests commodity-cycle modelling, asset-level operating risk, capital intensity and project finance discipline.

The most important analytical lesson is that a low leverage ratio in a strong earnings year does not automatically mean low through-cycle risk. Credit quality depends on the stability and convertibility of earnings into cash, the claims on that cash, the location and availability of liquidity, and management's response when conditions deteriorate.

**Portfolio conclusion:** All three merit a positive preliminary credit assessment, but for different reasons and with different stress triggers. A sound credit committee recommendation must explain not only why an issuer can repay, but what could impair repayment capacity and how much financial flexibility remains in that downside case.
