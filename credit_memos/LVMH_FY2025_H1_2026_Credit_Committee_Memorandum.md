# LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton — Corporate Credit Memorandum

**Assessment date:** 19 November 2026  
**Reporting currency:** EUR  
**Primary financial period:** FY2025 audited results, supplemented by H1 2026 interim financial report and Q3 2026 trading update  
**Purpose:** Public-information corporate credit case study for a banking portfolio  
**Status:** Preliminary analytical assessment; not a formal credit rating, credit approval, investment recommendation or representation of any lender's internal view.

---

## 1. Executive credit view

**Preliminary conclusion: Strong credit profile; suitable for consideration for senior unsecured corporate exposure, subject to ordinary counterparty, legal-entity and facility-level due diligence.**

LVMH combines global scale, a portfolio of leading luxury maisons, substantial recurring operating cash generation and access to diversified capital markets. At 31 December 2025, the Group reported €6.857bn of net financial debt and €68.949bn of equity, equivalent to net financial debt/equity of 9.9%. FY2025 operating free cash flow was €11.333bn.

The main credit consideration is not near-term solvency but earnings cyclicality and the Group's ability to preserve cash generation through a prolonged luxury-demand downturn. FY2025 revenue declined 5% reported (1% organically), while profit from recurring operations fell 9%. The latest nine-month 2026 update showed organic growth of 2%, but reported revenue remained 2% lower year on year and Fashion & Leather Goods remained weak, with a 5% organic decline in Q3.

Liquidity is a clear strength. At 30 June 2026, LVMH reported €12.202bn of cash, cash equivalents and current available-for-sale financial assets, plus €10.8bn of undrawn confirmed credit lines. These resources provide a substantial liquidity buffer against €5.6bn of outstanding short-term negotiable debt securities at that date. The Group's published long-term ratings were AA- (S&P) and Aa3 (Moody's), both with stable outlooks, as displayed on its investor debt-and-ratings page.

**Credit stance:** Positive / strong capacity, with monitoring focused on fashion and leather goods performance, working-capital absorption, capital returns, acquisition appetite, foreign-exchange movements and short-term funding rollover.

## 2. Company overview and business model

LVMH is a global luxury group operating across five principal business groups:

- Wines & Spirits;
- Fashion & Leather Goods;
- Perfumes & Cosmetics;
- Watches & Jewelry; and
- Selective Retailing.

Its portfolio includes internationally recognised maisons and retail businesses. The portfolio structure provides exposure to several product categories and customer occasions, rather than reliance on a single label or product line. The breadth of the portfolio is a credit strength, although the business groups are not fully independent: many remain exposed to common drivers such as global wealth, consumer confidence, tourism, foreign exchange, brand desirability and discretionary spending.

At FY2025, Fashion & Leather Goods generated €37.770bn of revenue, approximately 46.7% of Group revenue (€37.770bn / €80.807bn). This makes the division a material earnings concentration despite the Group's broader portfolio. The credit analysis should therefore distinguish revenue diversification from profit diversification: Fashion & Leather Goods generated €13.209bn of the Group's €17.755bn profit from recurring operations in 2025, approximately 74.4%.

## 3. Business risk assessment

### Strengths

1. **Brand equity and pricing power.** Established luxury maisons, heritage, craftsmanship and controlled distribution can support premium pricing and customer loyalty. These attributes may provide more resilience than mass-market discretionary retail, but they do not eliminate cyclical demand risk.
2. **Portfolio breadth.** The Group operates across fashion, leather goods, wines and spirits, beauty, jewellery, watches and selective retail. FY2025 organic performance varied by division: Watches & Jewelry grew 3% and Selective Retailing grew 4%, while Fashion & Leather Goods declined 5%.
3. **Global scale and distribution.** LVMH's international reach supports access to multiple customer markets and tourism flows. It also creates foreign-exchange, geopolitical, regulatory and regional-demand exposure.
4. **Strong cash generation.** FY2025 operating free cash flow was €11.333bn, up 8% year on year, despite weaker revenue and operating profit.

### Risks

1. **Luxury demand cyclicality.** Luxury purchases are discretionary and can be deferred when consumer confidence, financial markets or wealth effects weaken.
2. **Fashion & Leather Goods concentration.** The division represented approximately 46.7% of FY2025 revenue and 74.4% of profit from recurring operations. A prolonged slowdown in this segment could have a disproportionate effect on Group earnings.
3. **China and travel-related exposure.** The Q3 2026 update specifically described a less buoyant environment in China, while the United States, Europe and Japan saw good growth. Changes in Chinese consumer confidence, local luxury demand and international shopping patterns remain relevant.
4. **Brand and reputation risk.** Product quality, creative direction, authenticity, sustainability, labour practices and reputational events can affect demand and pricing power.
5. **Inventory and working capital.** Luxury groups must manage inventory carefully to protect scarcity, product relevance and full-price sell-through. Weak demand can increase inventory days, discounting risk and working-capital requirements.
6. **Foreign exchange.** The Group reports across multiple currencies. Currency movements can materially affect reported revenue, profit and net debt even where underlying local-currency trading is stable.

**Business risk view:** Strong competitive positioning and portfolio breadth, offset by meaningful discretionary-demand cyclicality and a material Fashion & Leather Goods earnings concentration.

## 4. Financial performance

### FY2025 versus FY2024

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | €84.683bn | €80.807bn | -5% reported |
| Organic revenue growth | — | -1% | — |
| Profit from recurring operations | €19.571bn | €17.755bn | -9% |
| Recurring operating margin | 23.1% | 22.0% | -1.1 percentage points |
| Net profit, Group share | €12.550bn | €10.878bn | -13% |
| Operating free cash flow | €10.478bn | €11.333bn | +8% |
| Net financial debt | €9.228bn | €6.857bn | -26% |
| Equity | €69.287bn | €68.949bn | broadly stable |

The reported revenue decline included a negative currency effect. Organic revenue, measured on a constant consolidation scope and currency basis, declined 1%. Profit from recurring operations fell faster than revenue, indicating margin pressure. Nevertheless, operating free cash flow increased and net financial debt declined, demonstrating cash conversion and balance-sheet capacity during a softer trading year.

### H1 2026 update

For the six months ended 30 June 2026, LVMH reported:

- Revenue of €38.644bn, down 3% reported and up 2% organically;
- Profit from recurring operations of €8.691bn, down 4%;
- Recurring operating margin of 22.5%;
- Operating free cash flow of €4.100bn, up 2%;
- Net financial debt of €8.245bn, compared with €6.857bn at 31 December 2025.

The H1 increase in net financial debt is important: cash and current available-for-sale financial assets declined by €1.3bn, while gross borrowings after derivatives were broadly stable. The interim report attributes the movement in part to equity-related transactions. This is a reminder that shareholder distributions and share purchases can reduce liquidity even when operating cash generation remains strong.

### Nine-month 2026 trading update

Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was €57.148bn, down 2% reported but up 2% organically. Q3 organic growth was 1%. By business group, nine-month organic performance was +5% for Wines & Spirits, -2% for Fashion & Leather Goods, flat for Perfumes & Cosmetics, +8% for Watches & Jewelry and +6% for Selective Retailing.

This is a mixed recovery rather than a uniform rebound. Fashion & Leather Goods recorded a 5% organic decline in Q3, which warrants continued monitoring because of its importance to Group earnings.

## 6. Liquidity and refinancing risk

Liquidity is a principal credit strength.

At 30 June 2026, the Group reported €12.202bn of cash, cash equivalents and current available-for-sale financial assets and €10.8bn of undrawn confirmed credit lines. Outstanding short-term negotiable debt securities under the NEU CP and USCP programmes were €5.6bn. The undrawn confirmed lines were therefore approximately 1.93x the outstanding short-term negotiable debt securities (€10.8bn / €5.6bn). This is a simple coverage comparison, not a full liquidity coverage ratio; it does not assume that every line is available to every issuer or legal entity under all circumstances.

LVMH also maintains a €30bn Euro Medium Term Note programme and euro- and US-dollar commercial paper programmes. The investor debt-and-ratings page lists outstanding bond issues with maturities across 2027–2036, including a number of fixed-coupon euro bonds. The maturity schedule should be refreshed at each review date and reconciled to the consolidated debt note.

**Key refinancing questions for a lender:**
- What proportion of commercial paper is backed by committed facilities and what are the draw conditions?
- Are cash balances and facilities freely transferable between the relevant issuing and operating entities?
- What are the next 24-month contractual principal and interest payments, including leases?
- What are the Group's committed acquisition, capex and shareholder-return requirements?
- What are the currency, interest-rate and derivative exposures by legal entity?

No conclusion on exact covenant headroom is made because a lender-certified covenant calculation and facility-level covenant package have not been established from the public information reviewed.

## 7. Cash flow and debt service capacity

FY2025 operating free cash flow of €11.333bn exceeded FY2025 net financial debt of €6.857bn. This comparison illustrates the Group's strong cash generation relative to its net debt stock, but it is not a debt-service coverage ratio: operating free cash flow is an annual flow, net debt is a point-in-time balance, and the Group's definition of operating free cash flow should be retained when comparing periods.

For H1 2026, operating free cash flow was €4.100bn. The Group defines this measure to include operating investments and repayment of lease liabilities as components of operating activities. In H1 2026, operating cash flow was €7.647bn, operating investments were €2.061bn and lease principal repayments were €1.486bn. The measure should not be compared directly with a conventional free cash flow measure that excludes lease principal repayments without reconciling the definitions.

The key downside transmission mechanism is likely to be a combination of lower operating profit, adverse working-capital movements and reduced cash conversion, rather than an immediate inability to access funding.

## 8. Indicative downside analysis

The following is a transparent sensitivity, not management guidance or a forecast. It applies a 20% reduction to FY2025 profit from recurring operations while holding net financial debt constant at the FY2025 level.

| Item | FY2025 base | Illustrative downside |
|---|---:|---:|
| Profit from recurring operations | €17.755bn | €14.204bn |
| Net financial debt | €6.857bn | €6.857bn |
| Net debt / recurring operating profit | 0.39x | 0.48x |

The ratio rises from approximately 0.39x to 0.48x. This suggests considerable headline balance-sheet capacity under this narrow sensitivity. However, it is not a complete stress test: it does not model cash taxes, interest, working capital, lease obligations, acquisitions, dividends, buybacks, FX movements or changes in cash balances. A full downside case should project monthly liquidity and cash flow under a deeper and longer demand shock.

### More severe stress variables to model in a full build

- Revenue decline of 10%–15% over a 12-month period;
- Recurring operating margin compression of 200–400 basis points;
- Working-capital outflow from slower inventory turnover;
- Reduced asset disposals and weaker operating cash conversion;
- Higher refinancing costs on maturing debt and commercial paper;
- Continued dividends and share repurchases versus suspension or reduction.

The purpose of these variables is to test cash liquidity and debt repayment capacity, not to imply that these outcomes are expected.

## 9. Key credit risks and mitigants

| Risk | Potential credit impact | Mitigant / monitoring response |
|---|---|---|
| Prolonged luxury downturn | Lower revenue, margins and cash flow | Brand portfolio, geographic breadth, cost discipline; monitor organic sales and margins |
| Fashion & Leather Goods weakness | Disproportionate earnings pressure | Monitor segment revenue, recurring profit and inventory |
| China demand weakness | Regional sales and tourism-related pressure | Geographic diversification; monitor regional disclosures |
| Inventory build | Cash absorption and markdown risk | Monitor inventory growth against sales and gross margin |
| Shareholder distributions | Higher net debt and lower cash reserves | Track buybacks, dividends and liquidity policy |
| FX volatility | Reported earnings and debt volatility | Review hedging policy and currency debt composition |
| Commercial paper rollover | Short-term liquidity pressure in stressed markets | Confirmed undrawn lines and cash reserves; verify legal-entity availability |

## 10. Preliminary credit recommendation

**Recommendation: Positive preliminary credit view; consider senior unsecured exposure subject to normal credit approval and legal-entity due diligence.**

The rationale is:
- very large operating scale and globally recognised luxury brands;
- high recurring operating margins;
- strong operating free cash flow;
- moderate net financial debt relative to equity;
- substantial cash and confirmed undrawn liquidity;
- published investment-grade ratings at the high end of the rating spectrum.

The principal reservation is the cyclical and concentrated nature of luxury earnings, particularly the contribution from Fashion & Leather Goods. A lender should not rely solely on headline net debt or brand strength. The analysis should be updated with full-year 2026 audited results when available and should incorporate the exact borrower entity, guarantees, ranking, security, debt maturity schedule and committed liquidity.

### Conditions / information required before a real credit decision

1. Latest audited consolidated and relevant entity-level financial statements.
2. Current debt schedule by borrower, currency, instrument, maturity, coupon and security.
3. Facility agreements, guarantees, negative pledge provisions and covenant definitions.
4. Latest lender covenant certificates and compliance calculations, where applicable.
5. Monthly 12–18 month liquidity forecast with base and downside cases.
6. Cash location, restrictions, trapped cash and facility drawability by legal entity.
7. Derivative and hedging disclosures, including counterparty exposures.
8. Capital allocation policy covering dividends, buybacks, acquisitions and capex.
9. Inventory ageing, stock obsolescence and working-capital trends.
10. Current rating-agency reports and any subsequent rating actions.
