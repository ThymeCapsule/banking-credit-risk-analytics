# Credit Committee Memorandum — ALS Limited (ASX: ALQ)

**Review date:** FY2026 information, as at 31 March 2026 (with disclosed post-balance-date refinancing through 8 May 2026)  
**Currency:** AUD unless stated otherwise  
**Purpose:** Illustrative corporate credit assessment based on public disclosures  
**Status:** Analytical case study; not a lender credit approval or investment recommendation

---

## 1. Executive credit view

**Indicative stance: Supportive, subject to full facility-level due diligence.**

ALS enters FY2026 with improving operating performance, lower net debt, strong reported covenant metrics, a largely fixed-rate debt profile and more than A$580 million of available liquidity. Net debt fell A$354.6 million year on year to A$1,069.5 million, while company-reported leverage declined from 2.3x to 1.5x and interest cover increased from 9.1x to 13.5x.

The strongest credit positives are (i) diversified testing and inspection activities across Life Sciences and Commodities, (ii) improving margins and cash generation, (iii) substantial covenant headroom on the company's disclosed definitions, and (iv) staggered note maturities extending to 2034.

The principal risks are (i) exposure to mining and exploration activity through Commodities, (ii) acquisition and integration execution in Life Sciences, (iii) foreign-exchange exposure arising from a global operating and funding footprint, and (iv) the material balance-sheet weight of intangible assets. The FY2026 result is strong, but the cyclical contribution from Commodities should not be extrapolated without a downside case.

**Credit committee conclusion:** The public information supports a positive preliminary credit view. It does not support an unconditional lending decision because facility size, purpose, tenor, security, borrower entity, cash-flow forecasts, full facility agreements and lender-specific covenant certificates have not been supplied.

## 2. Borrower and business profile

ALS is a global testing, inspection and certification business with two reportable operating segments:

- **Life Sciences:** environmental, food, pharmaceutical, consumer product and related analytical testing.
- **Commodities:** geochemistry, metallurgy, mining-related testing, coal quality, oil and lubricants, and other services supporting the resources lifecycle.

FY2026 revenue was A$3,320.1 million, comprising Life Sciences revenue of A$2,025.9 million (61.0%) and Commodities revenue of A$1,294.2 million (39.0%). Revenue was generated across Asia-Pacific (A$949.9 million), Europe/Middle East (A$1,168.3 million), the Americas (A$1,127.1 million) and Africa (A$74.8 million).

The mix provides sector and geographic diversification, although the Commodities division remains exposed to mining investment, exploration budgets, commodity prices and customer activity. Life Sciences provides a different demand base, but performance is not uniform across environmental, food and pharmaceutical end markets.

## 3. Operating and financial performance

| Metric | FY2026 | FY2025 | Credit interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue | A$3,320.1m | A$2,999.4m | Growth of 10.7% |
| Statutory NPAT | A$318.7m | A$256.2m | Growth of 24.4% |
| Underlying NPAT | A$381.2m | A$303.0m | Growth of 25.8%; non-IFRS |
| Adjusted underlying EBITDA | A$730.8m | A$620.2m | Pre-IFRS16 covenant-aligned measure |
| Underlying EBIT | A$599.0m | A$502.3m | Growth of 19.3% |
| Net debt | A$1,069.5m | A$1,424.1m | Reduction of A$354.6m |
| Net operating cash flow | A$485.7m | A$409.6m | Improved cash generation |

Commodities revenue increased 18.8% and underlying segment EBIT increased 25.9% to A$381.5 million. Life Sciences revenue increased 6.0% and underlying segment EBIT increased 10.2% to A$296.5 million. Commodities therefore generated the larger segment EBIT contribution in FY2026, making the group more exposed to the resources cycle than revenue mix alone suggests.

The company reported cash conversion of 92% and cash generated from operations of A$694.4 million. Net operating cash flow was A$485.7 million after net interest and tax payments. These figures support debt-servicing capacity, but ongoing capital expenditure, working-capital requirements, dividends and acquisitions must be considered when assessing cash available for debt reduction.

## 4. Leverage and capital structure

At 31 March 2026:

- Loans and borrowings (excluding leases): A$1,219.2 million.
- Cash and cash equivalents: A$149.7 million.
- Net debt excluding leases: A$1,069.5 million.
- Lease liabilities: A$352.7 million.
- Total equity: A$1,705.0 million.
- Company-reported net debt / adjusted underlying EBITDA: 1.5x.

The reported 1.5x leverage ratio uses adjusted underlying EBITDA of A$730.8 million on a pre-IFRS16 basis. It is the relevant company-reported covenant-aligned measure; it should not be replaced with statutory EBITDA or an analyst-created denominator.

The company also reports book gearing of 38.5%, down from 52.4% in FY2025. The improvement reflects the equity raising and reduction in net debt. Lease liabilities remain material and should be considered in a broader lease-adjusted leverage review, even though the disclosed financial covenants exclude lease interest and use a pre-IFRS16 EBITDA basis.

## 5. Debt maturity and liquidity

The company reported drawn debt of A$1,219.2 million against total facility limits of A$1,753.7 million. Available liquidity exceeded A$580 million at 31 March 2026.

The disclosed drawn debt maturity profile includes:

| Instrument / tranche | Maturity | Drawn amount |
|---|---|---:|
| USD bank facility | May 2026 | A$28.5m |
| USD bank facility | May 2028 | A$92.0m |
| EUR bank facility | August 2029 | A$16.7m |
| CHF bank facility | August 2029 | A$36.4m |
| US private placement notes | November 2028 | A$217.3m |
| US private placement notes | November 2030 | A$281.4m |
| US private placement notes | July 2032 | A$285.1m |
| US private placement notes | July 2034 | A$259.3m |

The May 2026 USD50 million revolving tranche was replaced after year-end by a new bilateral revolving facility of USD50 million (A$72.8 million). The company reported a pro forma weighted-average debt maturity of 4.0 years after this refinancing, compared with 3.9 years at the reporting date.

Approximately 90% of drawn debt was fixed rate and 10% floating rate at 31 March 2026. The weighted-average interest rate on drawn debt was 3.6%. This limits immediate sensitivity to floating-rate increases, although refinancing rates, foreign-exchange movements and future funding conditions remain relevant.

**Liquidity assessment:** Positive on disclosed information. The combination of cash, undrawn committed facilities and staggered maturities provides a meaningful liquidity buffer. A lender would still need to review facility availability conditions, expiry dates, currency matching, cash restrictions, refinancing documentation and the monthly liquidity forecast.

## 6. Covenant position

The annual report discloses common financial covenants for bank loans and long-term notes:

| Covenant | Requirement | FY2026 reported measure | Simple arithmetic headroom |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Times interest cover | Greater than 3.75x | 13.5x | 9.75x |
| Financial leverage | Less than 3.25x | 1.5x | 1.75x |

The covenant definitions are specific:
- Interest cover = underlying EBITDA on a pre-IFRS16 basis divided by net interest expense on interest-bearing loans and borrowings, excluding lease interest.
- Financial leverage = net debt divided by underlying EBITDA on a pre-IFRS16 basis.

The reported FY2026 ratios are described by the company as aligned with external loan covenant calculations. The headroom shown above is a simple comparison of reported ratios with stated thresholds. It is not a lender-certified covenant calculation and does not account for any adjustments, cure rights, testing dates or definitions in individual facility agreements.

## 7. Downside sensitivity

The following is an analyst-selected mechanical sensitivity, not company guidance or a probability-weighted forecast.

**Assumptions:** adjusted EBITDA declines 20%; finance costs increase 20%; net debt remains constant; no management actions, asset sales, refinancing, tax effects or working-capital offsets.

| Metric | FY2026 reported | Mechanical downside | Covenant threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net leverage | 1.50x | 1.88x | Below 3.25x |
| Interest cover | 13.50x | 9.00x | Above 3.75x |

Under these simplified assumptions, the company retains numerical headroom to both disclosed covenant thresholds. This is not a full covenant forecast: the actual covenant calculation may include contractual adjustments, debt movements, FX effects, acquisitions, disposals and test-period definitions. A proper lender model should flex revenue, segment margins, working capital, capex, interest rates, FX and debt balances through an integrated forecast.

## 8. Principal credit risks and mitigants

| Risk | Assessment | Evidence / mitigant | Required diligence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commodities cyclicality | Medium | Commodities delivered strong FY26 growth, but earnings are linked to mining and exploration activity. | Test lower exploration volumes, pricing pressure and commodity-cycle normalisation. |
| Life Sciences execution | Medium | Broad end-market base; FY26 segment revenue grew 6.0%. Environmental and pharmaceutical businesses had mixed conditions. | Review acquisition integration, Nuvisan performance and segment-level margins. |
| Refinancing and liquidity | Low-to-medium on disclosed data | >A$580m available liquidity, 4.0-year pro forma weighted-average maturity, staggered notes to 2034. | Confirm committed availability, facility expiry, covenants, refinancing terms and liquidity forecast. |
| Interest-rate risk | Moderate / mitigated | 90% fixed-rate drawn debt; weighted-average borrowing rate 3.6%. | Confirm hedge counterparties, hedge tenor, break costs and refinancing assumptions. |
| Foreign-exchange risk | Medium | Global revenue and debt are diversified across multiple currencies; some debt is issued in currencies aligned to operating cash flows. | Review natural hedges, FX sensitivities, derivative positions and translation effects. |
| Intangible asset / impairment risk | Medium | Intangible assets of A$1,610.3m are material relative to total assets of A$3,902.5m. | Review goodwill allocation, impairment headroom and downside assumptions. |
| Capital allocation | Medium | Debt reduction was strong, but dividends and investment compete with deleveraging. | Review dividend policy, acquisition appetite, capex commitments and leverage targets. |

## 9. Preliminary credit recommendation

**Recommendation: Proceed to full due diligence; maintain a positive preliminary credit stance.**

The FY2026 public disclosures indicate a borrower with improving earnings, lower leverage, substantial reported covenant headroom, strong operating cash flow and a diversified funding profile. These factors support further consideration for a conventional senior lending facility, subject to the transaction's purpose, structure and risk appetite.

No facility amount, tenor, pricing, security package or final approval is recommended in this memorandum because those terms depend on information not available in public disclosures.

### Conditions precedent to a lender decision

1. Obtain the proposed facility request, purpose, amount, tenor, repayment profile and legal borrower details.
2. Obtain audited financial statements, latest management accounts and a three-year integrated forecast.
3. Obtain the executed facility agreements, covenant definitions, compliance certificates and any waivers or amendments.
4. Reconcile all debt, leases, guarantees, security interests, derivatives and restricted cash across the group.
5. Review monthly liquidity forecasts, committed undrawn facility availability and refinancing plans.
6. Run integrated downside cases covering commodity demand, segment margins, FX, interest rates, working capital and capex.
7. Assess group structure, guarantor coverage, intercompany arrangements, structural subordination and enforceability of security.
