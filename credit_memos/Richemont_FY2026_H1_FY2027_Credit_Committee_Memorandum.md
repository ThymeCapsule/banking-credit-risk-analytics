# Compagnie Financière Richemont SA — Corporate Credit Memorandum

**Assessment date:** 19 November 2026  
**Reporting currency:** EUR  
**Primary annual period:** FY2026, year ended 31 March 2026  
**Latest interim period:** H1 FY2027, six months ended 30 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Public-information corporate credit case study for a banking portfolio  
**Status:** Preliminary analytical assessment; not a formal credit rating, credit approval, investment recommendation or representation of any lender's internal view.

---

## 1. Executive credit view

**Preliminary conclusion: Very strong financial capacity, supported by a substantial net cash position, strong jewellery economics and positive operating momentum. Senior unsecured exposure could be considered subject to ordinary legal-entity, facility and liquidity due diligence.**

Richemont's credit profile is supported by a portfolio of globally recognised jewellery and watchmaking maisons, a high-margin jewellery business, strong operating cash generation and a material net cash position. At 31 March 2026, the Group reported net cash of €8.496bn. At 30 September 2026, net cash remained substantial at €7.729bn, while H1 FY2027 operating cash flow was €2.5bn and reported free cash flow was €2.028bn.

Trading momentum strengthened in H1 FY2027. Sales were €12.282bn, up 16% at actual exchange rates and 17% at constant rates. Operating profit was €3.129bn, up 33%, although this included €229m of positive one-time tariff-related items. Excluding those items, operating profit was €2.9bn. Jewellery Maisons generated €9.146bn of sales and a 35.3% operating margin (33.4% excluding positive one-time items). Specialist Watchmakers' margin improved to 8.8%, while the Other business area remained loss-making at €12m.

The principal credit risks are concentration in jewellery, exposure to discretionary luxury demand and tourism, elevated inventory, precious-metal input costs, foreign exchange and the lower profitability of the watchmaking and Other businesses. Inventory was €10.262bn at 30 September 2026, 7% above the prior-year period, although inventory rotation improved to 16.1 months of cost of sales from 18.1 months.

**Credit stance:** Strong capacity and substantial liquidity. Monitor inventory quality and conversion, jewellery demand, watchmaking profitability, shareholder distributions, acquisitions and the sustainability of the net cash buffer.

## 2. Company overview and business model

Richemont is a Switzerland-based luxury group with three principal business areas:

- **Jewellery Maisons:** Buccellati, Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and Vhernier.
- **Specialist Watchmakers:** including A. Lange & Söhne, IWC Schaffhausen, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Panerai, Piaget and Vacheron Constantin.
- **Other:** primarily Fashion & Accessories maisons and related businesses, including Alaïa, Chloé, Delvaux, Montblanc, Peter Millar and others.

The Group's economic profile is increasingly weighted towards jewellery. In FY2026, the Jewellery Maisons generated €16.5bn of sales, up 8% at actual rates and 14% at constant exchange rates, and achieved a 30.5% operating margin. Specialist Watchmakers' sales declined 4% at actual rates (up 1% at constant rates), with a 3.4% operating margin. The Other business area generated an operating loss of €96m.

The distinction between business areas matters for credit. Jewellery contributes the majority of Group sales and a disproportionately high share of operating profit. The watchmaking business has a lower margin and a more uneven recovery profile, while Other remains loss-making. Richemont is diversified by maison, geography and channel, but not evenly diversified by profit pool.

## 3. Business risk assessment

### Strengths

1. **Leading jewellery maisons.** Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and the Group's other jewellery brands benefit from heritage, design identity, craftsmanship and established global distribution. In H1 FY2027, Jewellery Maisons sales grew 19% at constant rates.
2. **High jewellery profitability.** H1 FY2027 Jewellery Maisons operating margin was 35.3%, or 33.4% excluding positive one-time items. This is the key earnings and cash-generation engine.
3. **Geographic reach.** H1 FY2027 sales grew at constant rates in the Americas (+23%), Europe (+13%), Asia Pacific (+16%) and Japan (+30%); Middle East & Africa declined 2%. Regional breadth reduces dependence on one market, although demand remains sensitive to global wealth and travel.
4. **Direct-to-client distribution.** Direct-to-client sales represented 78% of H1 FY2027 sales, up 200 basis points year on year. Greater control over retail distribution can support customer relationships, brand presentation and gross economics, although it also requires store investment and fixed operating costs.
5. **Strong balance sheet.** Net cash of €7.729bn at 30 September 2026 provides a meaningful buffer against demand and funding shocks.

### Risks

1. **Jewellery profit concentration.** The jewellery division is the core earnings engine. Any damage to brand desirability, product relevance or high-end jewellery demand could disproportionately affect Group profitability.
2. **Luxury demand and wealth sensitivity.** Purchases are discretionary and can be affected by consumer confidence, financial-market performance, tourism and geopolitical uncertainty.
3. **Inventory intensity.** Inventory was €10.262bn at 30 September 2026, equivalent to 16.1 months of cost of sales. Long production cycles, precious stones, metals and high-value finished goods can tie up substantial capital. Inventory ageing, product obsolescence and resale/markdown assumptions require review.
4. **Precious-metal and gemstone costs.** Higher gold and other raw-material prices can compress margins or require price increases. Price increases may not be fully passed through without affecting volumes.
5. **Watchmaking volatility.** Specialist Watchmakers' H1 FY2027 operating margin was 8.8%, well below jewellery. Recovery in this segment is positive but does not yet provide the same earnings cushion.
6. **Other business-area losses.** Other reported an H1 FY2027 operating loss of €12m. The Group must continue to manage restructuring, brand investment and profitability in this portfolio.
7. **Foreign exchange and tariffs.** Richemont has significant currency exposures. It reported hedging 70% of forecast net cash-flow exposure on a rolling 12-month basis, increasing to 100% in the two-month window. Hedging reduces but does not eliminate translation, timing and basis risks.
8. **Retail fixed-cost base.** A large boutique network and direct-to-client strategy support brand control but add lease, staffing and occupancy commitments.

**Business risk view:** Strong competitive positioning and exceptional jewellery economics, offset by discretionary demand, earnings concentration and material inventory investment.

## 4. Financial performance

### FY2026 audited results

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | €21.399bn | €22.420bn | +5% reported; +11% constant FX |
| Gross profit | €14.319bn | €14.438bn | +1% |
| Gross margin | 66.9% | 64.4% | -250 bps |
| Operating profit | €4.467bn | €4.492bn | +1% |
| Operating margin | 20.9% | 20.0% | -90 bps |
| Profit from continuing operations | €3.762bn | €3.464bn | -8% |
| Profit for the year | €2.750bn | €3.484bn | +27% |
| Cash flow generated from operating activities | €4.443bn | €4.880bn | +€437m |
| Net cash | €8.257bn | €8.496bn | +€239m |

The increase in total profit for the year should be interpreted carefully: profit from continuing operations declined 8%, while total profit increased 27%, partly because the prior year included a €1.012bn loss from discontinued operations associated with YNAP. For ongoing credit assessment, continuing operating performance and cash generation are more informative than the headline total-profit growth rate.

FY2026 gross margin declined 250 basis points to 64.4%, reflecting foreign-exchange movements, higher raw-material costs and other operating pressures. Operating margin declined 90 basis points to 20.0%. The Group nevertheless generated €4.880bn of operating cash flow and ended the year with €8.496bn of net cash.

- Specialist Watchmakers: sales €1.706bn; operating result €150m; margin 8.8%, or 7.7% excluding positive one-time items.
- Other: sales approximately €1.4bn; operating loss €12m.

## 5. Leverage and capital structure

Richemont reported a net cash position rather than net debt at both FY2026 year-end and H1 FY2027.

At 31 March 2026, the Group's net cash calculation included:
- Cash at bank and on hand: €8.522bn;
- Investments in externally managed and money-market funds: €8.713bn;
- Borrowings: (€4.490bn);
- Bank overdrafts: (€4.249bn);
- Net cash: €8.496bn.

The Group's net cash definition includes cash and cash equivalents, investments in externally managed bond and money-market funds, and external borrowings including corporate bonds. It should not be confused with gross cash alone, and bank overdrafts are included as borrowings in the reconciliation.

At 30 September 2026, the interim presentation reported net cash of €7.729bn, compared with €6.519bn at 30 September 2025. The net cash balance declined by €767m from March 2026, despite positive H1 free cash flow. The movement should be reconciled to dividends, other shareholder distributions, foreign exchange, investment disposals/acquisitions and other financing movements before drawing conclusions about underlying cash retention.

**Credit interpretation:** The net cash position provides considerable balance-sheet flexibility. However, the reduction from March to September shows why cash-flow analysis must include capital allocation and financing flows rather than relying on the operating cash-flow headline alone.

## 6. Liquidity and refinancing risk

Liquidity is a major credit strength. Richemont had €8.496bn of net cash at 31 March 2026 and €7.729bn at 30 September 2026. The FY2026 annual report details the cash and investment components of net cash and the related borrowings and overdrafts.

The interim presentation's summary balance sheet at 30 September 2026 showed:
- Current assets: €27.980bn;
- Current liabilities: €8.763bn;
- Equity: €23.462bn;
- Net cash: €7.729bn.

A simple current-assets/current-liabilities ratio is approximately 3.19x (€27.980bn / €8.763bn). This is an analyst-calculated broad balance-sheet indicator, not a conventional quick ratio: current assets include €10.262bn of inventory and other items that may not be immediately realisable at carrying value. The strong net cash position is a more direct liquidity indicator, subject to restrictions and legal-entity location.

**Key refinancing questions for a lender:**
- What are the gross contractual debt and overdraft maturities by entity and currency?
- Which cash and money-market investments are unrestricted and immediately available?
- Are there material committed but undrawn facilities, and what are their draw conditions?
- How much of the Group's liquidity is held at operating subsidiaries versus the parent?
- What are the lease maturity profile and other fixed contractual obligations?
- What are the proposed dividend and capital-allocation commitments?

No lender covenant headroom is calculated because the public disclosures reviewed do not establish a relevant lender facility covenant package or lender-certified covenant calculations.

## 7. Cash flow, inventory and debt-service capacity

FY2026 cash flow generated from operating activities was €4.880bn, up from €4.443bn. H1 FY2027 operating cash flow increased to €2.500bn from €1.854bn. H1 FY2027 free cash flow was €2.028bn, including €408m of lease principal payments and a €332m net inflow from changes in other non-current assets. The company-defined free cash flow should be used consistently and should not be assumed to be identical to every lender's cash flow available for debt service definition.

Working capital remains a key variable. H1 FY2027 changes in working capital represented a €940m cash outflow, compared with €792m in the prior-year period. Inventory was €10.262bn at 30 September 2026, up 7% year on year, but reported inventory rotation improved from 18.1 to 16.1 months of cost of sales.

This is a mixed signal: inventory grew, but more slowly than sales and with better rotation. The credit review should still request inventory ageing, category-level stock data, impairment policies and evidence of sell-through, particularly for watches and fashion/accessories.

Capital expenditure in H1 FY2027 was €400m, around 3.3% of sales. Approximately 48% was allocated to distribution, 36% to manufacturing and 16% to technology and other items. Investment supports the maisons' long-term competitiveness but is a recurring use of cash.

## 8. Indicative downside analysis

The following sensitivity is illustrative, not management guidance or a forecast. It applies a 25% reduction to H1 FY2027 operating profit excluding the €229m positive one-time items, annualised mechanically for comparison. This is a simple earnings sensitivity only; it is not a full liquidity forecast.

| Item | H1 FY2027 adjusted reference | Illustrative 25% downside |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operating profit excluding positive one-time items | €2.900bn | €2.175bn |
| Net cash at 30 September 2026 | €7.729bn | €7.729bn before stress cash effects |

The sensitivity indicates that earnings could decline materially while the Group would still begin with a substantial net cash buffer. However, it does not model the impact of a demand shock on inventory, receivables, supplier terms, tax, dividends, acquisitions, foreign exchange or lease payments. A proper downside case should project quarterly cash flow and net cash through a prolonged luxury downturn.

### More severe stress variables for a full build

- Jewellery sales decline of 10%–15% from the current run rate;
- Lower jewellery gross margin from gold/precious-stone costs and discounting;
- Watchmaker margins remaining below historical levels;
- Inventory days increasing and requiring write-downs;
- Cash outflow from working capital and currency hedges;
- Continued dividends and acquisitions during weaker trading;
- Reduced liquidity in money-market investments or higher counterparty haircuts.

These are stress-testing assumptions, not predictions.

## 9. Key credit risks and mitigants

| Risk | Potential credit impact | Mitigant / monitoring response |
|---|---|---|
| Jewellery demand slowdown | Material decline in Group operating profit | Leading maison portfolio, high margins and substantial net cash; monitor jewellery sales and margin |
| Inventory accumulation | Working-capital absorption and potential write-downs | Improved rotation to 16.1 months; request ageing and sell-through data |
| Gold and gemstone inflation | Margin compression or higher prices | Pricing discipline and supplier management; monitor gross margin and volumes |
| Watchmaking weakness | Low-margin segment remains a drag | H1 recovery in sales and margin; monitor sustained profitability |
| Other business losses | Continued drag on Group earnings | Restructuring and portfolio management; track segment break-even |
| FX and tariffs | Reported margin and cash-flow volatility | Rolling FX hedging programme; review hedge effectiveness and tariff pass-through |
| Shareholder distributions | Erosion of net cash | Strong cash generation; monitor dividend and buyback policy |
| Retail fixed costs and leases | Reduced operating flexibility during demand shocks | Direct-to-client channel and brand control; analyse lease commitments and store productivity |

## 10. Preliminary credit recommendation

**Recommendation: Strong preliminary credit view; consider senior unsecured exposure subject to standard credit approval, legal-entity analysis and facility-level due diligence.**

The rationale is:
- a globally recognised portfolio of luxury maisons, particularly leading jewellery brands;
- high operating margins and strong profitability in the Jewellery Maisons;
- positive H1 FY2027 sales and earnings momentum;
- substantial net cash of €7.729bn at 30 September 2026;
- positive operating cash flow and free cash flow;
- diversified geographic and direct-to-client distribution.

The principal reservations are inventory intensity, jewellery profit concentration, watchmaking and Other segment profitability, and the risk that shareholder distributions or acquisitions reduce the net cash buffer. A lender should not assume that all reported cash and investments are immediately available to every borrowing entity.

### Conditions / information required before a real credit decision

1. Latest audited consolidated statements and entity-level financials for the proposed borrower and guarantors.
2. Gross debt and overdraft schedule by legal entity, currency, maturity, interest rate and security.
3. Cash and investment location, restrictions, counterparty exposure and transferability.
4. Facility agreements, guarantees, negative pledge provisions and covenant definitions.
5. Latest lender covenant certificates, where applicable.
6. Monthly 12–18 month liquidity forecast with base and downside cases.
7. Inventory ageing, product-category mix, impairment and sell-through information.
8. Lease liability maturity profile and store commitments.
9. Currency derivative positions, hedge counterparties and collateral requirements.
10. Dividend, buyback, acquisition and capex commitments.
11. Current rating-agency reports and subsequent rating actions.
