# BHP Group Limited — Corporate Credit Memorandum

**Assessment date:** 19 November 2026  
**Reporting currency:** US dollars (US$)  
**Primary financial period:** FY2026, year ended 30 June 2026  
**Latest operating update:** Q1 FY2027, quarter ended 30 September 2026  
**Purpose:** Public-information corporate credit case study for a banking portfolio  
**Status:** Preliminary analytical assessment; not a formal credit rating, credit approval, investment recommendation or representation of any lender's internal view.

---

## 1. Executive credit view

**Preliminary conclusion: Strong corporate credit profile, supported by a globally competitive asset base, substantial operating cash generation and low reported leverage. The key credit variables are commodity-price volatility, execution of a large investment programme, operational continuity and the durability of cash generation through a commodity downturn.**

BHP reported FY2026 revenue of US$58.760bn, underlying EBITDA of US$32.947bn, net operating cash flow of US$21.778bn and net debt of US$8.694bn. Net debt/underlying EBITDA was 0.3x and gearing was 13.4%. Free cash flow was US$9.8bn, up 83% year on year. These metrics indicate substantial capacity to absorb a moderate cyclical downturn at the FY2026 reporting date.

Copper became BHP's largest earnings contributor, generating US$18.2bn of underlying EBITDA, or 54% of Group underlying EBITDA, with a 70% segment margin. Iron ore generated US$14.5bn of underlying EBITDA at a 61% margin. The portfolio is therefore concentrated in two large bulk and base-metal earnings engines, although the mix of copper, iron ore, steelmaking coal and energy coal provides some diversification.

The balance-sheet improvement needs to be interpreted in context. FY2026 net debt declined by US$4.2bn from FY2025, supported by strong operating cash flow, a US$4.3bn Antamina silver-streaming transaction, US$0.8bn of asset-sale proceeds and capital management. BHP also paid US$6.8bn in dividends to its shareholders and US$2.4bn to non-controlling interests, and funded US$2.0bn of Samarco settlement obligations (BHP equity share). The silver-streaming proceeds are a material capital-management transaction, not recurring operating cash generation.

Q1 FY2027 shows a mixed operational picture. Copper production fell 19% year on year to 400.7kt, while the average realised copper price rose 53% to US$7.01/lb. Iron ore production increased 4% to 66.5Mt, but the realised iron ore price declined 9% to US$76.27/wmt. Steelmaking coal production was broadly flat, with realised prices up 34%. FY2027 production guidance remained unchanged. This demonstrates why credit analysis must distinguish price effects from volume, grade, cost and reliability effects.

**Credit stance:** Strong preliminary capacity, with a low starting leverage position. A lender should stress-test commodity prices, cash costs, sustaining and growth capex, dividends, joint-venture commitments, closure and remediation liabilities, and the potential for project overruns. Do not extrapolate FY2026 peak earnings or the 0.3x leverage ratio as a through-cycle outcome.

## 2. Company overview and business model

BHP is a diversified global resources company with operations and investments across copper, iron ore, steelmaking coal, energy coal and the developing potash business. Its principal earnings sources are large-scale, long-life mines and processing assets, supported by integrated logistics and marketing capabilities.

The FY2026 earnings mix was led by:
- **Copper:** US$18.2bn underlying EBITDA, 54% of Group underlying EBITDA; 70% underlying EBITDA margin.
- **Iron ore:** US$14.5bn underlying EBITDA; 61% underlying EBITDA margin.
- **Steelmaking and energy coal:** smaller earnings contribution, with differing demand and transition profiles.
- **Potash:** Jansen is a major growth investment, with first production targeted for mid-CY2027.

Copper's share of Group earnings rose from 45% in FY2025 to 54% in FY2026. This is strategically attractive given electrification, grid investment and data-centre-related demand, but it also increases the importance of copper price, ore grade, recovery rates, project execution and Chilean operational performance.

BHP's iron ore business remains a significant cash generator. Western Australia Iron Ore (WAIO) recorded 265Mt of attributable production in FY2026 and maintained a low-cost position. Steelmaking coal is more exposed to steel-cycle conditions and energy transition risk. Energy coal is being managed towards closure, with NSW Energy Coal expected to cease mining by FY2030.

## 3. Business risk assessment

### Strengths

1. **Scale and asset quality.** Large, long-life operations, integrated infrastructure and established customer relationships support cost competitiveness and operating resilience.
2. **Low-cost iron ore.** WAIO maintained a leading cost position and achieved record production and shipments in FY2026. Low-cost assets are generally better positioned to remain cash generative through weaker commodity cycles.
3. **Copper growth exposure.** Copper generated more than half of FY2026 underlying EBITDA. BHP's portfolio and project pipeline provide exposure to structural demand from electrification, power infrastructure and industrial development.
4. **Strong operating cash generation.** FY2026 net operating cash flow was US$21.8bn and free cash flow was US$9.8bn, despite capital and exploration expenditure of US$10.3bn.
5. **Financial flexibility.** Net debt/underlying EBITDA of 0.3x and gearing of 13.4% provide a substantial starting buffer.
6. **Portfolio and geographic diversification.** Operations across Australia, Chile, Peru and Canada reduce reliance on a single mine or jurisdiction, although the portfolio remains exposed to common global commodity cycles.

### Risks

1. **Commodity-price volatility.** Earnings and cash flow are sensitive to copper, iron ore and metallurgical coal prices. A sustained fall in realised prices can rapidly reduce EBITDA and increase leverage.
2. **Ore grade and operational reliability.** Copper production is affected by planned grade declines, maintenance, weather and processing complexity. Q1 FY2027 copper output fell 19% year on year.
3. **Capital intensity.** FY2026 capital and exploration expenditure was US$10.3bn; FY2027 expenditure was expected to be approximately US$11bn. Growth projects compete with dividends and balance-sheet protection for cash.
4. **Project execution.** Jansen Stage 1 is a large project with an estimated project expenditure of US$8.4bn and target first production in mid-CY2027. Jansen Stage 2 is estimated at US$6.9bn, with first production targeted for late FY2031. Delays, cost escalation or weaker potash prices could reduce returns and absorb cash.
5. **Joint-venture and non-operated commitments.** BHP's growth pipeline includes investments and commitments in non-operated joint ventures, including Vicuña and Resolution. The Group must manage capital calls, partner alignment and project governance.
6. **Safety and social licence.** Fatal incidents, contractor safety, community relationships, Indigenous rights and permitting can affect production, reputation, costs and project approvals. The Q1 FY2027 report disclosed fatal incidents at Escondida and Peak Downs.
7. **Environmental and closure liabilities.** Tailings, water, rehabilitation, closure and emissions obligations can create long-dated cash costs and contingent exposures.
8. **Geopolitical, tax and regulatory risk.** Mining assets are exposed to royalty, tax, permitting, labour, trade and local-content changes across jurisdictions.
9. **Foreign exchange and input costs.** A stronger Australian dollar, Chilean peso movements, diesel, energy, freight and labour inflation can affect unit costs. Management's unit-cost guidance is based on stated exchange-rate assumptions.
10. **Shareholder distributions.** Dividend payments are discretionary but can compete with debt reduction and investment during weaker commodity conditions.

**Business risk view:** High-quality, globally competitive assets and strong cost positions, balanced against pronounced commodity cyclicality, high capital requirements and operational/project risks.

## 5. Leverage and capital structure

At 30 June 2026, BHP reported:
- Net debt: US$8.694bn.
- Underlying EBITDA: US$32.947bn.
- Net debt / underlying EBITDA: 0.3x.
- Gearing ratio: 13.4%.

The reported net debt ratio is low. It is also measured against a strong commodity-price year. For a lender, a through-cycle leverage assessment should use a conservative commodity-price deck and consider the impact of sustaining capital expenditure, closure obligations, leases, joint-venture commitments and shareholder distributions.

BHP's stated net debt target range is US$10bn–US$20bn. FY2026 net debt of US$8.7bn was below the bottom of this range. This indicates capacity to fund growth and shareholder returns, but the target range is a capital-management preference, not a covenant or minimum liquidity requirement.

### Drivers of the FY2026 net debt reduction

BHP stated that stronger operating cash flow and active capital management, including US$4.3bn of proceeds from the Antamina silver-streaming transaction and US$0.8bn from asset sales, more than offset:
- US$6.8bn of dividends paid to BHP shareholders;
- US$2.4bn of dividends paid to non-controlling interests; and
- US$2.0bn of Samarco settlement obligations (BHP equity share).

The silver-streaming transaction should be treated as a financing/asset monetisation-type source of liquidity rather than recurring operating cash flow. It transfers an economic interest in future silver production in exchange for upfront proceeds. The credit analyst should assess the effect on future by-product credits and asset cash flows.

In August 2026, after the FY2026 reporting date, Global Infrastructure Partners provided US$2.0bn for a 49% stake in a partnership relating to BHP's share of WAIO inland power consumption. This is a subsequent transaction and should not be included in the 30 June 2026 net debt calculation. BHP stated that it retains operational control of WAIO and ownership of the inland power infrastructure.

## 6. Liquidity and refinancing risk

The low net debt balance and strong operating cash generation indicate a sound consolidated liquidity position. However, net debt alone does not establish the amount of immediately available cash at each borrowing entity.

The public summary used for this memorandum does not reproduce a complete legal-entity cash availability and debt maturity schedule. A formal lender analysis should extract the contractual maturity profile, committed undrawn facilities, restricted cash, guarantees, security and cross-default provisions from the annual report and debt-investor disclosures.

**Key lender diligence questions:**
- What are gross borrowings, cash and liquid investments by currency and legal entity?
- What proportion of cash is restricted, trapped, pledged or held in joint ventures?
- What is the contractual debt maturity ladder over the next five years?
- Which committed facilities are available, and what are the material draw conditions?
- What are the interest-rate, currency and commodity hedging positions?
- What are the lease, rehabilitation, closure, litigation and Samarco-related payment profiles?
- What are the guarantees, security packages and structural subordination exposures across the Group?

BHP's public rating disclosures in the FY2026 results release state Moody's A1 (stable) / P-1 and Fitch A (stable) / F1 for long-term / short-term ratings respectively. Ratings are subject to agency review and should be checked again at the time of any actual credit decision.

## 7. Cash flow, capital expenditure and debt-service capacity

FY2026 net operating cash flow was US$21.778bn. Capital and exploration expenditure was US$10.257bn, while BHP reported free cash flow of US$9.8bn. These figures demonstrate significant cash generation after investment, although BHP's free cash flow definition should be checked and consistently applied rather than assumed to equal a lender's cash flow available for debt service.

Capital expenditure is expected to increase. BHP indicated FY2027 capital and exploration expenditure of approximately US$11bn and medium-term average annual sustaining capital expenditure of approximately US$7.00 per tonne for the relevant iron ore measure, excluding specified projects and programmes. The distinction between sustaining and growth capital is important: sustaining expenditure is needed to preserve existing production, while growth expenditure is discretionary only to a degree because projects may involve long lead times, contractual commitments and strategic dependencies.

Jansen is the most visible large growth project:
- Stage 1 estimated project expenditure: US$8.4bn; 87% complete in the Q1 FY2027 operational review; first production targeted for mid-CY2027.
- Stage 2 estimated project expenditure: US$6.9bn; 18% complete; first production targeted for late FY2031.

The project expenditure figures include project capital expenditure, project operating expenditure, right-of-use asset construction costs and related contingencies, as defined in the operational review. They should not be treated as the remaining cash to spend. A lender should obtain the latest cost-to-complete, committed spend, contingency and ramp-up forecasts.

## 8. Indicative downside analysis

The following is a transparent sensitivity, not a management forecast or a full integrated model. It holds net debt constant at US$8.694bn and reduces FY2026 underlying EBITDA by 25% and 40%. This isolates the effect of lower earnings on the leverage ratio; it does not model cash outflows, working capital, capex deferrals, dividends, taxes or asset impairments.

| Scenario | Underlying EBITDA | Net debt | Net debt / EBITDA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2026 reported base | US$32.947bn | US$8.694bn | 0.26x |
| 25% EBITDA downside | US$24.710bn | US$8.694bn | 0.35x |
| 40% EBITDA downside | US$19.768bn | US$8.694bn | 0.44x |

The sensitivity suggests that the starting leverage ratio remains low under a substantial earnings shock, assuming net debt does not increase. That assumption is deliberately favourable and incomplete. In a severe downturn, lower prices could coincide with weaker operating cash flow, working-capital movements, fixed sustaining capex, project commitments and pressure to reduce or suspend shareholder distributions.

### Full stress case: variables to model

A proper credit model should combine:
- Copper price and production/grade downside;
- Iron ore price and shipment-volume downside;
- Steelmaking coal price and cost volatility;
- Diesel, energy, freight, labour and foreign exchange shocks;
- Higher sustaining capex to maintain mine and infrastructure reliability;
- Jansen and copper project cost overruns or delays;
- Joint-venture cash calls and Samarco/closure obligations;
- Dividend reductions or continuation under the capital allocation framework;
- A liquidity reserve and minimum cash threshold.

Commodity price shocks should be applied to realised prices and sales volumes separately. A single percentage reduction in EBITDA is a useful first-pass sensitivity but not a substitute for asset-level modelling.

## 9. Key credit risks and mitigants

| Risk | Potential credit impact | Mitigant / monitoring response |
|---|---|---|
| Copper and iron ore price decline | Lower EBITDA and operating cash flow; leverage rises | Low-cost asset base and diversified commodity mix; stress a through-cycle price deck |
| Copper grade and operational disruption | Lower production and fixed-cost absorption | Multiple operating assets and unchanged FY27 guidance; monitor recovery, maintenance and grade |
| High capital expenditure | Reduced free cash flow and slower deleveraging | Strong operating cash generation; distinguish sustaining from growth spend |
| Jansen project execution | Cost overruns, delayed cash generation and increased funding needs | Stage 1 nearing completion; require cost-to-complete and ramp-up updates |
| Joint-venture commitments | Additional cash calls and contingent funding needs | Portfolio partnerships; review contractual obligations and partner credit |
| Safety and social licence | Shutdowns, litigation, regulatory scrutiny and reputational damage | Safety systems and public reporting; monitor investigations and corrective actions |
| Environmental and closure obligations | Long-dated cash outflows and potential provisions | Asset-level planning and rehabilitation programmes; review independent estimates |
| Shareholder distributions | Cash leakage during weaker commodity markets | Capital allocation framework and ability to adjust distributions; test payout flexibility |
| FX, diesel and energy costs | Unit-cost inflation and margin pressure | Cost discipline and hedging; stress exchange rates and input prices |

## 10. Preliminary credit recommendation

**Recommendation: Strong preliminary credit view; senior unsecured exposure may be considered subject to normal credit approval, entity-level analysis, facility documentation and downside liquidity testing.**

The rationale is:
- a globally competitive asset portfolio with leading positions in copper and iron ore;
- FY2026 underlying EBITDA of US$32.9bn and net operating cash flow of US$21.8bn;
- net debt of US$8.7bn and reported net debt/underlying EBITDA of 0.3x;
- strong free cash flow after capital expenditure;
- diversified operations and a substantial project pipeline.

The principal reservations are commodity cyclicality, the price sensitivity of FY2026 earnings, large investment requirements, project and operational execution, and potential cash demands from shareholder distributions and long-dated obligations. The 0.3x leverage ratio should not be interpreted as a through-cycle forecast or covenant measure.

### Conditions / information required before a real credit decision

1. Latest audited consolidated financial statements and legal-entity accounts for the proposed borrower and guarantors.
2. Gross debt, cash, liquid investments and committed facilities by entity and currency.
3. Five-year contractual debt maturity schedule, including bonds, loans, leases and overdrafts.
4. Facility agreements, guarantees, security, negative pledge and covenant definitions.
5. Current rating-agency reports and any rating actions after the FY2026 release.
6. Monthly 18–24 month liquidity forecast under base and downside commodity assumptions.
7. Asset-level realised price, production, unit-cost and sustaining-capex forecasts.
8. Jansen Stage 1 and Stage 2 cost-to-complete, commitments, contingency and ramp-up cases.
9. Non-operated joint-venture commitments, capital calls and guarantees.
10. Rehabilitation, closure, tailings, environmental, litigation and Samarco obligations.
11. Commodity, foreign-exchange, interest-rate and energy hedging positions.
12. Dividend and capital-allocation policy under downside scenarios.
