# Debt maturity and covenant analysis

## Scope

This module extracts FY2026 public disclosures for SGH Limited (30 June 2026), ALS Limited (31 March 2026) and Lindsay Australia Limited (30 June 2026). Values are in AUD millions unless stated otherwise.

The maturity CSV records contractual undiscounted cash flows by maturity bucket. These are not the same as balance-sheet carrying values. Lease liabilities are kept separate from bank borrowings and notes.

## Debt maturity observations

- **SGH:** Reported drawn-facility weighted average maturity of 4.6 years. Principal cash flows (including derivatives) are A$97.4m / A$265.9m / A$750.0m / A$715.2m for variable-rate debt and A$206.3m / A$145.6m / A$755.0m / A$949.5m for fixed-rate debt across within 1 year / 1–2 years / 2–5 years / over 5 years. The annual report also discloses coupon and derivative cash flows separately; those are not included in the maturity CSV's principal-only schedule.
- **ALS:** Long-term notes are weighted toward later maturities, with A$583.6m due in the 2–5 year bucket and A$586.2m over 5 years. Bank loan contractual cash flows total A$193.4m, with A$36.6m due within 12 months. Lease liabilities are separately disclosed. ALS reported weighted average debt maturity of 3.9 years at 31 March 2026; after the 8 May 2026 refinancing, the pro forma weighted average maturity was 4.0 years.
- **Lindsay Australia:** The bank-loan contractual cash flow schedule includes A$8.093m within 1 year, A$8.093m in 1–2 years and A$124.640m in 2–5 years. The large 2–5 year bucket should be read alongside the disclosed variable finance facility's annual review feature; it is not a guarantee that the facility cannot be reviewed or repriced earlier. Equipment finance and lease cash flows are separately included.

## Covenant disclosures

### ALS Limited

The annual report discloses common financial covenants for bank loans and long-term notes:
- Times interest cover greater than 3.75x.
- Financial leverage less than 3.25x.

The company reported 13.5x interest cover and 1.5x net debt / adjusted underlying EBITDA for FY26. The covenant definitions use pre-IFRS16 underlying EBITDA and exclude lease interest from the interest-cover denominator. Simple arithmetic headroom is therefore 9.75x on interest cover and 1.75x on leverage. This is an analytical comparison of published ratios and thresholds, not a covenant certificate or confirmation of lender calculations.

### Lindsay Australia Limited

The annual report discloses:
- Gross leverage ratio below 3.50x.
- Interest cover ratio above 2.50x.
- Debt service cover ratio above 1.20x.

The company states that it complied with all required covenants during FY26 and did not identify circumstances indicating difficulty over the following 12 months. It does not provide the actual covenant calculation values in the reviewed note. The company's 1.92x net leverage and the model's 5.93x analyst interest-cover calculation are not used to infer covenant headroom because they are not established as matching the facility definitions.

### SGH Limited

The FY26 annual report provides drawn and undrawn facility maturity, liquidity and debt maturity information, but the reviewed report does not disclose financial covenant thresholds or covenant calculations. These fields are marked not disclosed rather than estimated.

## Analytical limitations

- Contractual cash flows include timing and, where applicable, interest or derivative payments; carrying values are accounting balances. Do not compare them as if they were the same measure.
- Foreign currency debt is translated into AUD by the company at reporting-date rates; FX movements can change reported AUD values.
- The reporting dates differ across issuers.
- Covenant headroom is calculated only where the reported ratio is sufficiently aligned with the disclosed covenant definition. No headroom is inferred from a non-comparable metric.
- Public disclosure does not provide complete facility agreements, covenant certificates, waiver terms, amortisation schedules or lender-specific definitions. This is not a substitute for lender due diligence.
